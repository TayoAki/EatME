# Weight Class Can Beat TapCook on Protein

TapCook: Budget Meal Plans is a dinners-only weekly planner that third-party estimates put at **$45–50K a month** without calling an AI at all, and its own reviewers are asking for the calorie, protein and GLP-1 options that Weight Class already has. testmaxx LLC, an apparently one-person Vermont publisher, turned a failed recipe-saving app into a 19-step quiz that ends at a hard paywall, then picks dinners from a fixed catalogue with an optimiser on the phone and totals one grocery list from "a national reference price for each ingredient" adjusted for chain and region. It earns money only from subscriptions, **$9.99 a month or $29.99 a year**, sold by TikTok creators paid per trial or subscription through referral codes, and it holds **4.76 stars from about 7,100 US ratings**. Its weakness is trust: the 177 written US reviews average **2.54 stars**, 41% of them about the paywall, trial or billing, others about a thin catalogue, prices "always way off on the meats" and the absence of any low-calorie or high-protein option. At least 61 imitators hold fewer ratings between them than TapCook alone, grocers from Instacart to Kroger give away planners priced from their own shelves, and no big calorie tracker plans to a grocery budget. The data question has an unexpectedly good answer: no retailer licenses a storable price table, but USDA's public-domain Purchase to Plate prices are keyed to the FNDDS food codes that Weight Class's database also uses and cover **3,573 of its 5,432 foods**, which reproduces TapCook's method with a named source. AI belongs nowhere near the numbers, since language-model meal plans fall **roughly 280–700 kcal a day** short of their targets or of dietitians' plans in 2026 studies, and none has been tested against shelf prices. The binding guardrails are Apple's ban on prices in store metadata, the FTC's demand that savings and health claims be proven before they run, and evidence that budget pressure layered on calorie targets compounds eating-disorder risk among people already short of food. Weight Class should build the feature, but as a cost layer on its deterministic Plan tomorrow, protein per dollar for one person, priced from USDA data and sold inside Premium, rather than as a family dinner planner that would have to out-shout TapCook on TikTok.

## TapCook turns a quiz and TikTok codes into $50K a month

### A dinners-only planner that never calls an AI

TapCook began as a different app. testmaxx LLC released it on 17 July 2026 as "TapCook: Make What You Scroll", a saver of TikTok and Instagram recipes whose bundle ID still reads `com.testmaxx.reelchef`, and five weeks later version 2.0 announced "a completely new app": "Set a budget and TapCook builds the week to come in under it… The recipe-saving features have been retired" ([iTunes Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6769601354&country=us); [Social Growth Engineers](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/budget-meal-planner-app-hits-50k-mrr-in-3-months); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/id6769601354)). The seller sits at 64 Shea Drive in South Burlington, Vermont, has no other app on its Apple account and filed the TAPCOOK trademark itself; reviewers describe a single developer who "responded quickly like about 10 minutes", and no founder or funding was found ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.testmaxx.reelchef&hl=en_US); [Apple developer lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1848498396&entity=software&country=us); [Trademarkia](https://www.trademarkia.com/tapcook-50079075); [App Store review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml)).

The product is a long quiz followed by a plan. ScreensDesign's recording counts **19 onboarding steps**, opening with an App Tracking Transparency prompt and running through ZIP code, a grid of four nearby stores, household size with a "kid-friendly dinners" toggle, cooking nights, a pantry inventory, a weekly budget slider, diets, appliances, allergies, dislikes, testimonials with a rating prompt and a referral code, before a loader reading "Pricing a cart of 16 items at Target" and a hard paywall ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/)); "Has you answer questions for 20 minutes then leads to the final unavoidable subscription," one reviewer wrote ([App Store review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml)). Subscribers get a week of dinners, never breakfast or lunch, each card priced per serving (Cottage Pie at $3.13, Sheet-Pan BBQ Drumsticks at $2.25); one aisle-sorted list totalled at the chosen chain ("YOUR GROCERY TOTAL $87.25 · 24 items left · Shaw's"); a Kitchen tab whose ticked staples come "straight off your grocery bill"; and buttons that send the list to Instacart, DoorDash and, since 7 November, Walmart ([iTunes Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6769601354&country=us); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/id6769601354)). Twenty releases in 78 days added household sharing, frozen leftovers carried into later weeks and, on 31 October, Canada ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/id6769601354)).

Nothing in the plan comes from a language model at run time. "Planning and pricing run on your device using recipe and price data shipped inside the app," say the terms, and the privacy policy adds, "We do not send your plan, grocery list, kitchen or answers to any AI service" ([Terms](https://tapcook.co/terms); [Privacy](https://tapcook.co/privacy)). Prices "start from a national reference price for each ingredient and are adjusted for the chain and region you choose", only "some chains have had prices checked against the shelf", and the website promises "whole packs" at "regular prices. Never a sale" ([iTunes Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6769601354&country=us); [Terms](https://tapcook.co/terms); [tapcook.co](https://tapcook.co/)), but TapCook never says where the reference prices or the factors come from. Nutrition is disclaimed rather than offered: no current screen shows calories or macros, and any figures "are rough estimates calculated from generic ingredient data" from an app that "is not a source of dietary, nutritional, medical or fitness advice" ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/); [Terms](https://tapcook.co/terms)). The catalogue is the bottleneck. The website claims "hundreds of weeknight dinners", reviewers report "Only 12 recipes offered" and "the same 9 over and over" once filters bite ([tapcook.co](https://tapcook.co/); [App Store review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml)), and the design explains why: a fixed pool costs nothing per plan and works offline, but it shrinks fast under Paleo, no-pork or kid-friendly filters.

### Creator codes sell a $29.99 yearly plan

TapCook earns money only from subscriptions. The US store lists **TapCook Monthly at $9.99 and TapCook Yearly at $29.99**, with a seven-day free trial on the yearly plan alone, and "building and viewing your weekly meal plans requires a subscription" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/id6769601354); [iTunes Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6769601354&country=us)). The monthly plan "has no free trial and bills immediately", returning users are billed at purchase, and the paywall preselects Yearly under "Try for Free" ([Terms](https://tapcook.co/terms); [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/)). One subscription covers a household, the yearly price "works out to about $2.50 a month", the app shows no third-party ads, and whether its store buttons earn commission is not disclosed ([tapcook.co](https://tapcook.co/); [Privacy](https://tapcook.co/privacy)). Against Weight Class Premium at $39.99 a year or $7.99 a month ([PLAN.md](../PLAN.md)), TapCook is $10 cheaper on the year and $2 dearer on the month.

The commercial record is strong, though every revenue figure is an estimate. Social Growth Engineers reported **$50K in monthly recurring revenue** and about 100,000 downloads on 26 October, three months after launch; ScreensDesign shows "$45k/mo", mwm.ai more than 250,000 downloads, and Google Play a 50K+ install bracket and "#2 top grossing food & drink" ([Social Growth Engineers](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/budget-meal-planner-app-hits-50k-mrr-in-3-months); [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/); [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/tapcook-make-what-you-scroll/6769601354); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.testmaxx.reelchef&hl=en_US)). At $2.50 a month, $50K would mean about 20,000 subscribers if all paid yearly, and fewer with monthly payers in the mix. On 9 November the app held **4.76 stars from about 7,100 US ratings** and ranked **#22 in US Food & Drink**, and Canada, opened nine days earlier, ranked #5 ([iTunes Lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6769601354&country=us); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/id6769601354); [App Store, Canada](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/id6769601354)).

Growth comes from TikTok. Social Growth Engineers credits "a budget challenge and a supermarket name hook" with "5M+ views across small accounts" ([Social Growth Engineers](https://www.socialgrowthengineers.com/budget-meal-planner-app-hits-50k-mrr-in-3-months)), and the privacy policy confirms both engines: paid TikTok ads measured through the TikTok Business SDK and SKAdNetwork, and a creator programme in which a code entered during onboarding is recorded "so the creator can be paid for referring you" once the user starts a trial or subscribes ([Privacy](https://tapcook.co/privacy)). Creators tagged #tapcookpartner or #tapcookad test the app against "a $35 grocery budget" or "$78.56 at Whole Foods", and TikTok now auto-generates search pages such as "tapcook app subscription cost" ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/tapcook); [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/tapcook-app-subscription-cost)). Like MenuFit, TapCook is a distribution business first, but a cheaper one to run, since a catalogue and an on-device optimiser cost nothing per plan.

### Written reviews average 2.5 stars

The star average hides a different verdict. The 177 written US reviews posted between 11 August and 6 November average **2.54 stars** and **57.6% give one or two**, falling to 1.95 in early November ([review feed p.1](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml); [p.2](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml); [p.3](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml?l=en); [p.4](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=4/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml)), and mwm.ai shows the same split of 4.8 against 2.5 ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/tapcook-make-what-you-scroll/6769601354)). The likeliest cause is the rating prompt at onboarding step 16, before the paywall, when users are still hopeful ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/)); Apple disallows custom review prompts, though whether TapCook's is Apple's own sheet is unconfirmed ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). MenuFit showed the same gap ([MenuFit teardown](../research/menufit-teardown.md)), and Weight Class's rules forbid any rating prompt before a first good scan ([PLAN.md](../PLAN.md)).

Money dominates the complaints. **41% of written reviews concern the paywall, trial, billing or an undisclosed price**, including 64 of the 102 low-star ones: 32% object to the hard paywall ("Devs should at least let people see the first week without having to agree to a trial"), 9% report charges during a trial, after cancelling or twice, and 5% name the irony: "Get this app to save money on groceries when you're trying to cut costs via spending 10 dollars a month" ([App Store review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml)). The terms explain much of the trial anger, since the monthly plan bills at once and returning users get no trial ([Terms](https://tapcook.co/terms)). Repetition draws 15% of reviews ("Every week has AT LEAST 4 of the same recipes as the week before"); price accuracy draws only ten, nine of them saying the estimates miss, but pointedly: "it told me $116 total on the app and I copy and pasted in Instacart came out to $248", "it's always way off on the meats", and a list pricing eggs singly "yet stores sell eggs by the dozen" ([App Store review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml); [p.2](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml)). Recipes draw suspicion of AI authorship, from an instruction to "sharpen an egg" with "no cook times or temperatures" to "some of the chicken recipes come out undercooked according to the recipe time and temperatures" ([App Store review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml)).

The 9% of reviews about nutrition read like a brief for Weight Class: "Given the recent interest in GLP ones and low-calorie diets, why would they not offer an option for low-calorie meals", "make options for weight loss/gains", "Not a good variety for keto, low carb and high protein" ([App Store review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml)). The praise is for decision relief: "The nights of fighting about what's for dinner are OVER!" ([App Store review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=6769601354/sortby=mostrecent/xml)). One more seam shows in the disclosures, since Apple's privacy label declares no health or location data while the Google Play form declares both ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tapcook-budget-meal-plans/id6769601354); [Google Play data safety](https://play.google.com/store/apps/datasafety?id=com.testmaxx.reelchef&hl=en_US)). TapCook has proved that people want a priced week of dinners; its billing, its prices and its silence on nutrition are all beatable.

## Sixty-one imitators hold fewer ratings than TapCook alone

TapCook's format has been copied faster than it has been beaten. Thirty-four App Store searches on 9 November found **62 apps released since January 2025 that pitch a budget-led weekly plan with a grocery list, 50 of them since June 2026**; they hold 12,569 US ratings, 7,102 of them TapCook's, while the median imitator has two and the 33 released after TapCook hold 156 between them ([App Store search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=budget+meal+planner&entity=software&country=us&limit=50); [search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=budget+meal+plan&entity=software&country=us&limit=50); [search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=meal+plan+budget&entity=software&country=us&limit=50)). The count is a floor, since each search returns at most 50 apps. Only Potto, NeatEat, Whipp, Mealia and Pomelo have passed 100 ratings, plus the UK-focused mise ([mise](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6777471316&country=us)); the copy converges word for word ("Tell nomi who you're feeding, which nights you cook…") ([nomi](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6815107888&country=us)); and TapCook ranks first in search for "budget meal planner" ([App Store search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=budget+meal+planner&entity=software&country=us&limit=50)). The clones sell their price data as the difference, from prices "labelled as estimates, because they are" (Mealbird) to "real, current prices for your store" (Carby) and receipts that make "the prices you actually paid replace the estimates" (MealMaxx), and they draw the angriest reviews when the claim fails: "no prices, and no store specific things. The ad is a lie" ([Mealbird](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6794577462); [Carby](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6779585429); [MealMaxx](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6798084955); [Savor](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6788732141)). Several bolt on calorie logging, Potto's "Calorie Snap" among them ([Potto](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6778760884)), so budget planners are converging on trackers from their side.

The older field mostly prices nothing. ReciMe (330,295 ratings), AnyList (81,376), Paprika (54,278) and eMeals (52,804) own the big rating bases, but only eMeals sells plans that "meet your budget" and only AnyList lets shoppers type prices in ([ReciMe](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1593779280&country=us); [AnyList](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=522167641&country=us); [Paprika](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1303222868&country=us); [eMeals](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=575756462&country=us)), while Eat This Much, on the store since 2015, already pairs calorie targets with a budget input ([Eat This Much](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=981637806&country=us)), so the combination itself is not new. The sharpest warning is Albertsons-owned Mealime, which cut Pro to $2.99 a month in 2021 "to make Mealime Pro more accessible to everyone", then closed on 21 October 2026 and sent its users to a free "Meals Hub" inside Albertsons' grocery apps ([Mealime](https://www.mealime.com/lower-prices); [Mealime closing notice](https://www.mealime.com/closing)). That is where the field is heading. Instacart's Clementine builds a cart at the user's store and "surfaces deals and cheaper substitutes" so shoppers "stay on budget", Kroger's AI Shopping Assistant builds carts "around a budget and dietary needs", and Walmart pitches its Sparky assistant at meal plans within "budgetary limits", all free and all priced from the retailer's own shelf ([Canadian Grocer](https://canadiangrocer.com/instacarts-clementine-tackles-dinner-decision-fatigue-ai); [The Shelby Report](https://theshelbyreport.com/2026/07/29/kroger-launches-ai-shopping-assistant-across-all-banners/); [Fruit Today](https://fruittoday.com/en/walmart-introduces-sparky-a-generative-ai-assistant-to-plan-compare-and-shop-smarter/)).

Calorie trackers plan meals but never to a dollar budget. MyFitnessPal's Premium+, at $99.99 a year, builds weekly plans, syncs lists to Instacart, Walmart, Kroger, Amazon Fresh and Whole Foods, and mentions "budget-friendly options" without planning to a figure ([MyFitnessPal](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/premium-plus)); its purchases of the planner Intent in 2025 and of Cal AI in 2026 point towards planning, shopping and logging in one place ([Connect The Watts](https://connectthewatts.com/2025/02/14/myfitnesspal-acquires-intent-to-enhance-meal-planning-for-members/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens)). Lifesum and Yazio bundle meal plans, Noom and WeightWatchers sell meal planning inside dearer programmes, Lose It!'s "meal planning" is meal targets, and MacroFactor and Cal AI list no planner ([Lifesum](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=286906691&country=us); [Yazio](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=946099227&country=us); [Noom](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=634598719&country=us); [WeightWatchers](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=331308914&country=us); [Lose It!](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=297368629&country=us); [MacroFactor](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1553503471&country=us); [Cal AI](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6480417616&country=us)). Cost is the one step of that loop no tracker has built.

Demand is broad but cooling. r/EatCheapAndHealthy counted about 11.5 million members in late-2025 snapshots, against 120,000–240,000 for each large GLP-1 subreddit ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/EatCheapAndHealthy); [GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/Zepbound)), and 53% of adults called groceries a major source of stress in an August 2025 AP-NORC poll ([Good Morning America](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/food/story/people-searching-struggle-meals-grocery-stress-continues-125923994)). Yet food-at-home prices rose only **1.8%** in the year to September 2026 ([BLS](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/economicdata/cpi_10142026.pdf)), and USDA forecasts 2.3% for 2026 against a 2.6% twenty-year average ([USDA ERS](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-price-outlook/summary-findings)), so the app wave arrived after the peak of price anxiety. Protein is the exception: ERS expects beef and veal to rise **8.7%** in 2026 while eggs fall 29.3%, and September's average prices were $6.79 a pound for ground beef against $2.06 for a whole chicken, $1.67 for dried beans and $2.27 for a dozen eggs ([USDA ERS](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-price-outlook/summary-findings); [FRED, ground beef](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/APU0000703112); [chicken](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/APU0000706111); [dried beans](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/APU0000714233); [eggs](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/APU0000708111)). For Weight Class's first audience the frame shifts again: about 12% of US adults were taking a GLP-1 in late 2025, and households with a user cut grocery spending **5.3%** within six months of starting ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-health-tracking-poll-prescription-drug-costs-views-on-trump-administration-actions-and-glp-1-use/); [RFD-TV](https://www.rfdtv.com/glp-1-drugs-begin-to-reshape-u-s-food-spending?p=369); [Journal of Marketing Research](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437251412834)), so their job is meeting a protein target on a small appetite for less money, not cutting a family's bill.

Budget shoppers also resent paying to save. One Potto reviewer called "charging $10/week" "psycho", another asked "in what world would I want to pay $40 a month for poorly AI-generated recipes?" ([Potto](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6778760884)), and the long-lived winners are free or nearly so: SuperCook with no purchases, Paprika at $14.99 once, AnyList at $9.99 a year ([SuperCook](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1477747816); [Paprika](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1303222868&country=us); [AnyList](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id522167641)). People pay when the saving shows: "I set my weekly number and it sticks to it" ([yumzy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6782147501)). A budget feature inside an existing subscription sidesteps the second purchase decision, and Weight Class Premium already costs $60 a year less than MyFitnessPal Premium+.

## USDA gives away the price table that grocers will not license

No retailer offers what a budget planner most needs, a price table it may keep and rank. Kroger's API returns live store prices but forbids developers to "build databases, or otherwise create permanent copies"; Instacart's platform turns a list into an Instacart-hosted page priced at the user's store, returns only its URL and pays commission, but bans showing "priced items from multiple retailers on the same screen" and any use "to provide medical advice"; Walmart's affiliate agreement allows only Walmart-supplied prices refreshed within 24 hours and bans "price tracking"; and Amazon's Creators API requires an app's "principal purpose" to be advertising Amazon and bars using its content to "develop or improve large language or multimodal models" ([Kroger Developer Terms](https://developer.kroger.com/terms); [Instacart Developer Platform terms](https://docs.instacart.com/developer_platform_api/guide/terms_and_policies/developer_terms); [recipe docs](https://docs.instacart.com/developer_platform_api/guide/concepts/recipe); [Walmart affiliate terms](https://affiliates.walmart.com/terms); [Amazon Associates Program Policies](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies)). No public price API turned up for Albertsons, Costco or Aldi, and Target's terms of 10 September 2026 forbid scraping or storing "prices" ([Target terms](https://www.target.com/c/terms-conditions/-/N-4sr7l)). Scraping around those terms is a contract risk more than a criminal one: the Ninth Circuit held on 4 August 2026 that the user, not the maker of Perplexity's shopping agent, "accesses" Amazon's computers, but only under the anti-hacking statutes, while hiQ ended with a **$500,000** consent judgment for breaching LinkedIn's user agreement ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/08/ninth-circuit-holds-human-user-not-developer-of-ai-agent-responsible-for-websites-accessed/); [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/)). For an App Store app, guideline 5.2.2 settles it anyway by requiring permission under a third-party service's terms ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

The government already publishes the table. USDA's Economic Research Service builds its **Purchase to Plate National Average Prices** from Circana's retail scanner data, giving a price per 100 edible grams for each food code in FNDDS, the database behind the national dietary survey ([ERS Purchase to Plate](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/purchase-to-plate)). ERS says the prices can "estimate the national average cost of preparing a set of recipes", USDA used them to reevaluate the Thrifty Food Plan in 2021, and as a federal work the file carries no copyright ([ERS documentation](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/purchase-to-plate/documentation); [Copyright Office Compendium](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap300/ch300-copyrightable-authorship.pdf)). Weight Class's database is FNDDS 2021–2023 ([PLAN.md](../PLAN.md)), so the join is direct: matched on exact food codes, the latest public cycle (2017–18, 4,435 prices) covers **3,573 of Weight Class's 5,432 foods, or 65.8%**, and **4,163 (76.6%)** appear in at least one cycle ([Purchase to Plate prices](https://www.ers.usda.gov/media/6557/purchase-to-plate-national-average-prices-for-nhanes-csv-format.zip?v=99531); [fndds.json.gz](../data/fndds.json.gz)). The catches are age and reach: prices are in 2017–18 dollars, ERS advises against comparing cycles, the next cycle is due "no earlier than spring 2027", and the crosswalk and price tools stay restricted because they contain Circana's data ([ERS Purchase to Plate](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/purchase-to-plate); [ERS documentation](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/purchase-to-plate/documentation)). USDA's own remedy for age is a template. It updates the Thrifty Food Plan monthly by matching each basket category to a Consumer Price Index, eggs to the egg index and so on ([FNA cost reports](https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/cnpp/usda-food-plans/cost-food-monthly-reports)); BLS publishes those indexes with about 70 average food prices for the US and four regions ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/factsheets/average-prices.htm)); F-MAP's unit values for 90 food groups in 15 areas can supply regional factors ([ERS F-MAP](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-at-home-monthly-area-prices)); and the September 2026 Thrifty Food Plan, **$236.30 a week for a family of four** or $58.60 for a woman aged 20–50, gives honest budget anchors ([FNA cost reports](https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/cnpp/usda-food-plans/cost-food-monthly-reports)). That is TapCook's method, a national reference price adjusted by region, with the source named. Public data cannot supply TapCook's chain factor, and Weight Class should not pretend to one.

The other routes fail on storage or reach. Open Food Facts' Open Prices holds only **47,432 US prices from 394 stores**, 38% of them at Safeway, under a share-alike licence ([Open Prices dumps](https://prices.openfoodfacts.org/data/prices.jsonl.gz); [Open Prices data guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openfoodfacts/open-prices/main/docs/guides/data.md)); Spoonacular and Edamam forbid storing nearly everything they return ([Spoonacular terms](https://spoonacular.com/food-api/terms); [Edamam](https://developer.edamam.com/edamam-recipe-api)); and scanner and receipt panels are academic-only or sold to brands ([Kilts Center](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/research/kilts/research-data/nielseniq/policies); [Datasembly](https://datasembly.com/grocery-store-price-index-our-latest-resource/)).

Recipes are freer than prices, but the big free corpora are not. "A mere listing of ingredients or contents, or a simple set of directions, is uncopyrightable," says the Copyright Office, and courts treat ingredient lists as facts ([Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf); [Publications International v. Meredith](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/88/473/486900/); [Tomaydo-Tomahhdo v. Vozary](https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/15a0705n-06.pdf)), while expressive instructions, photographs and curated selections can be protected. Purely AI-generated material gets no copyright ([Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)), a position left standing when the Supreme Court declined to hear *Thaler v. Perlmutter* on 2 March 2026 ([Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/supreme-court-denies-certiorari-in-thaler-v-perlmutter-ai-cannot-be-an-author-under-the-copyright-act)), so AI-drafted recipes infringe no one by default but belong to no one unless people edit them. The corpora behind most recipe models are off-limits: RecipeNLG's 2.2 million recipes are for "non-commercial research and educational purposes" only, Recipe1M+ and Food.com were scraped with no commercial licence found, and Edamam "does not own license" for its two million web recipes ([RecipeNLG](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mbien/recipe_nlg); [Recipe1M+](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06553); [Food.com](https://paperswithcode.com/dataset/food-com-recipes-and-interactions); [Edamam](https://developer.edamam.com/edamam-recipe-api)). TapCook claims its own recipes and price data as testmaxx property and forbids scraping either ([Terms](https://tapcook.co/terms)).

Instacart's platform is the only retailer route that fits a "buy this list" button without storing prices, but its bans on multi-retailer price screens and medical advice keep it on the shopping list and away from GLP-1 guidance; Kroger's live prices could later serve as an optional check; and Amazon, scraping, Spoonacular, Edamam and the scraped recipe corpora stay out.

## AI plans fall hundreds of calories short and are untested on price

More than 20 peer-reviewed studies since 2023 agree on the direction of error. Language-model meal plans look plausible and often score as satisfactory on diet-quality indexes, but they **under-deliver energy**, skew protein high and carbohydrate or fat low, keep missing vitamin D, calcium, fibre and whole grains, and get their own nutrient arithmetic wrong, while 38 of the 40 studies in a 2026 scoping review used hypothetical scenarios rather than real patients ([AJCN scoping review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41317995/)). Newer models have not fixed it: in February 2026 ChatGPT-5.2, Claude 4.5 and Gemini 3 still set phenylalanine below the prescription in 36 of 48 plans ([PubMed 42884610](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42884610/)). No study has tested plans for GLP-1 users, a group already short of protein, with 88% below national protein guidelines in one 2026 conference abstract ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1123350)).

On cost the evidence is an absence: no peer-reviewed study has compared language-model grocery prices with shelf prices, and the benchmark and informal tests above put the errors anywhere from 13.5% to nearly three times the budget. Prices also move under any fixed figure. In the month to September 2026 beef fell 1.3% while fresh fruit rose 2.0% ([USDA ERS](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-price-outlook/summary-findings)), an Urban Institute analysis found a modest meal outran SNAP benefits in 99% of counties ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/07/18/snap-doesnt-cover-even-a-cheap-meal-in-99-of-counties)), and Consumer Reports found Instacart offering about three-quarters of the products it checked at different prices to different shoppers ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/money/questionable-business-practices/instacart-ai-pricing-experiment-inflating-grocery-bills-a1142182490/)), so for online grocery a single "actual" price may not exist. TapCook's misses come from unnamed reference prices and pack sizes; a model would only add error on top.

Food-safety failures cluster where generation is open-ended. Pak'nSave's Savey Meal-bot proposed an "aromatic water mix" of bleach and ammonia in 2023 and was reined in by limiting inputs to food items; Google's AI Overviews advised glue on pizza in 2024; and in 2025 a man was hospitalised with bromism after swapping salt for sodium bromide following a ChatGPT exchange ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2023/08/11/supermarket_reins_in_ai_recipebot); [Museum of Failure](https://museumoffailure.com/exhibition/savey-meal-bot); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/google-is-putting-more-restrictions-on-ai-overviews-after-it-told-people-to-put-glue-on-pizza-011316780.html); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/man-asked-chatgpt-cutting-salt-diet-was-hospitalized-hallucinations-rcna225055)). Allergen slips recur in a minority of plans, almond milk in a nut-free diet among them ([PubMed 37269717](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37269717/); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20230515/Robo-diets-can-ChatGPT-formulate-safe-dietary-advice.aspx)); models split compound ingredients such as pesto into parts with F1 scores of only 0.69–0.89 ([PubMed 40362801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40362801/)); and ChatGPT-4.0 mini gave valid food-safety references only 35% of the time ([Journal of Nutrition Education and Behavior](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1499404625001484)). No study has measured how often AI recipes fall below USDA's minimum temperatures of 165°F for poultry, 160°F for ground meat and 145°F plus a three-minute rest for whole cuts ([FSIS chart](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20120915114427/http:/www.fsis.usda.gov/PDF/Safe_Miminum_Internal_Temperature_Chart.pdf); [UMaine Extension](https://extension.umaine.edu/food-health/2024/03/01/internal-temperatures/)), an evidence gap rather than evidence of safety that TapCook's undercooked-chicken review makes concrete. Models do better as checkers than as authors, as the menu-screening result in the table shows.

The deterministic alternative is USDA's own. The Thrifty Food Plan is a quadratic optimisation that keeps the basket "as close to current consumption patterns as possible" within nutrient, food-group, calorie and cost limits, raising the cost limit in one-cent steps until a solution existed, at **$835.57 a month for a family of four** in June 2021 ([TFP 2021 report](https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/resource-files/TFP2021.pdf)). That objective is Plan tomorrow's principle, start from what people already eat, written at national scale. Newer methods fit on a phone: mixed-integer goal programming with whole servings (no "1.7 eggs") found a feasible plan in all 810 test cases, against 48% for hard-constraint integer programming, in under 100 milliseconds with the open-source HiGHS solver ([arXiv 2605.13849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13849)), and grounding a model in USDA data cut its calorie error against targets to 1.55–3.68% ([arXiv 2502.20601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20601)). Waste matters more than pennies: consumer food waste costs about **$728 a person a year** ([BioCycle](https://www.biocycle.net/epa-updates-data-on-cost-of-household-food-waste/)), the average household wastes **31.9%** of the food it acquires and households that shop with lists waste less ([Penn State](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/study-suggests-us-households-waste-nearly-third-food-they-acquire)), and the Thrifty Food Plan assumes only 5% ([TFP 2021 report](https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/sites/default/files/resource-files/TFP2021.pdf)). A planner that buys whole packs and reuses opened ones can save more than a perfect unit price could; TapCook's whole-pack promise is right, and its per-egg pricing shows how easily the execution slips.

**The numbers belong to code.** Across these studies the gains come from moving facts into a database, arithmetic into sums and choices under constraints into a solver. That leaves a model three optional jobs in a budget plan: turning wishes into filters, drafting recipe steps for a person to edit, and wording a reason from numbers code computed, with cooking temperatures and allergen flags inserted from fixed tables. It is What to order's design applied to groceries, and it keeps the person's budget, ZIP code and goals out of every prompt, as What to order keeps targets and weight out of its own ([WHAT-TO-ORDER.md](../WHAT-TO-ORDER.md)).

## Price claims, protein floors and food insecurity set the guardrails

Apple's rules bite hardest on money. Guideline 2.3.7 keeps "pricing information" and "unverifiable product claims" out of app names, subtitles, screenshots and previews, and the new 2.3.14 of 16 October 2026 bars "specific or discounted pricing information" from creative assets ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=92th7e5i)), so "meals under $3" or "save $200 a month" cannot appear in store metadata at all. The same update moved the ban on hidden features into a new guideline 2.5.19, aimed at features "that weren't included in the version of the app that was submitted to App Review" ([Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=vvzx6cul)), so a budget layer must ship switched on in the reviewed build, as What to order did ([WHAT-TO-ORDER.md](../WHAT-TO-ORDER.md)). Guideline 1.4.1 demands disclosed methodology for health claims, and Weight Class fixed its 21 October rejection by putting citations inside the app beside the numbers ([PLAN.md](../PLAN.md)), so a budget plan's protein target or GLP-1 meal advice needs its own Sources entry. Groceries are physical goods that must not go through in-app purchase (3.1.3(e)), a third-party service's content needs its permission (5.2.2), and data sent to a third-party AI needs disclosure and "explicit permission" (5.1.2(i)) ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ey6d8onl)). The nearest precedent, Apple's April 2026 removal of Cal AI, was about billing rather than nutrition: a weekly-equivalent price shown more prominently than the amount billed, a trial toggle that obscured auto-renewal and a second offer pushed after a decline ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/21/apple-cal-ai-app-store-removal/)).

The FTC asks for proof before the claim. Its pricing guides say a comparison price must not "appreciably exceed the price at which substantial sales of the article are being made in the area" ([16 CFR 233](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-233)), and its 2025 Workado order turned on an accuracy claim "not substantiated at the time the representation was made" ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-05-02/html/2025-07617.htm)), so a savings figure needs a stated baseline, stores, dates and typical results before it runs. Health benefits need "competent and reliable scientific evidence", which testimonials are not ([16 CFR 255](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-255)), and the FTC has already acted against a GLP-1 telehealth firm that misrepresented whether its price included the medication ([NextMed](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-07-17/html/2025-13407.htm)). Its July 2026 proposed policy statement on AI, not yet final, treats misstating how often a system errs as deceptive and warns against steering outputs towards "objectives other than those that consumers ask for or expect", which would cover ranking stores by commission rather than by the person's cost ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/07/2026-13628/policy-statement-concerning-the-suppression-of-accuracy-in-artificial-intelligence-systems); [full text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-07/html/2026-13628.htm)). Creator campaigns, TapCook's engine, carry duties of their own: paid creators must disclose the connection in the video itself rather than behind "more", a creator's reported saving is read as typical unless the usual result is disclosed, and rewards conditioned on positive reviews are banned, at **$53,088 per violation** in 2026 ([16 CFR 255](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-255); [16 CFR 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-465); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/15/2026-18853/civil-penalty-inflation-adjustments)). With the federal click-to-cancel rule vacated in 2025 and the old rule restored in February 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-12/html/2026-02866.htm)), ROSCA's demand for clear terms and express consent ([15 U.S.C. 8403](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title15/html/USCODE-2023-title15-chap110-sec8403.htm)) and state laws such as California's cancel button beside any save offer and Minnesota's bar on unsolicited save offers set the subscription rules ([Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/california-updates-its-automatic-renewal-law-again); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/auto-renewal-laws-2025-round-up)), so the simplest compliant design shows no in-app save offer at all.

FDA's rules bind labels, not apps. The "healthy" definition took effect on 28 April 2025 with compliance due on 25 February 2028, but it governs food labels rather than an app that sells no food ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/27/2024-29957/food-labeling-nutrient-content-claims-definition-of-term-healthy); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/02/25/2025-03118/food-labeling-nutrient-content-claims-definition-of-term-healthy)), and the nine major allergens, sesame included since 2023, make the sensible flag list ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/food-allergies-what-you-need-know)). Weight Class neither stores allergies nor calls food safe ([WHAT-TO-ORDER.md](../WHAT-TO-ORDER.md)), so a budget plan should flag "contains milk, egg" from its ingredients, tell people to check packages, and keep foods-to-skip as its only filter.

Budget pressure on top of calorie targets is the subtle risk. A 2025 review of 38 studies links diet and fitness apps with more disordered-eating symptoms, though not causally ([Body Image](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39671845/)); food insecurity independently raises the odds of binge eating 1.66-fold and of binge-eating disorder 2.70-fold ([International Journal of Eating Disorders](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37040535/)); food-pantry clients with the most severe food insecurity report more dietary restraint and compensatory behaviour ([International Journal of Eating Disorders](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28626944/)); and GLP-1 drugs may worsen eating-disorder pathology ([International Journal of Eating Disorders](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38135891/)). The audience is under strain: **13.7% of US households were food insecure in 2024**, the highest rate since 2014, in what USDA says is its last such report ([USDA ERS](https://ers.usda.gov/sites/default/files/_laserfiche/publications/113623/ERR-358.pdf); [Agri-Pulse](https://www.agri-pulse.com/articles/23962-last-usda-hunger-survey-shows-food-insecurity-at-10-year-high)), and SNAP's work rules now reach adults up to 64 ([AP via Hawaii News Now](https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2026/01/31/expanded-work-requirements-snap-are-kicking-more-states/)). A plan must never meet a budget by cutting portions or meals, never reward the cheapest or smallest day, keep calm mode's numbers hidden, and use dignified words: "lower-cost" rather than "cheap", and "people experiencing food insecurity" if the subject comes up ([Feeding America style guide](https://www.feedingamerica.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/Feeding_America_Partner_Style_Guide_February_2026.pdf)).

The 2025 joint advisory on GLP-1 nutrition sets the protein and floor rules. It proposes 1.2–1.6 g of protein per kg during active weight loss, or simply **80–120 g a day**, never below 0.4–0.5 g/kg and not 2 g/kg or more for long, with small meals every three to four hours, adequate fluids and fibre raised gradually, and it warns that micronutrient shortfalls grow "especially at energy intakes <1200 kcal/d for females and < 1800 kcal/d for males" ([joint advisory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12304835/)). It also counsels against red and processed meats and refined carbohydrates, which steers budget plans towards the eggs, beans, fish and yoghurt that Weight Class's weekly suggestion already names ([joint advisory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12304835/); [insights.ts](../src/shared/insights.ts)). Two existing settings deserve a look through this lens: the men's calorie floor is 1,500 kcal, below the advisory's 1,800 kcal line, and the AI plan allows protein up to 2.2 g/kg ([nutrition.ts](../src/shared/nutrition.ts)). A budget plan in GLP-1 mode should cite the 80–120 g target, favour micronutrient-dense staples for men planned between 1,500 and 1,800 kcal, and show a "talk to your clinician" note instead of supplement advice.

Privacy turns on precision and inference. California defines precise geolocation as within 1,850 feet and Washington and Maryland as within 1,750 ([CCPA](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/cppa_act.pdf); [RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010); [Maryland §14-4701](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4701&enactments=false)), so a ZIP code or a chosen store stays outside all three. Washington's My Health My Data Act counts information "derived or extrapolated from nonhealth information" as consumer health data, and its attorney general confirms that purchases used to infer health status qualify ([RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010); [Washington AG](https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy)), so a grocery list derived from a GLP-1 plan plausibly counts; Maryland allows such data only when "strictly necessary" and bans its sale ([Maryland §14-4707](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4707&enactments=false)). Any retailer hand-off should carry a plain item list with no user ID, goal or medication context, and budget, ZIP and pantry data should stay out of AI prompts unless the consent screen names them.

## A protein-per-dollar layer on Plan tomorrow beats TapCook for one person

**Verdict: build it, as a cost layer on Plan tomorrow rather than a TapCook clone.** Weight Class already owns the hard parts TapCook lacks. Plan tomorrow drafts a day from the person's own meals against their calorie target, protein target and floor, with no AI, scoring each day by its distance from the calorie target plus a protein shortfall weighted twice as heavily in GLP-1 mode ([NEXT-FEATURES.md](../NEXT-FEATURES.md); [day-draft.ts](../src/shared/day-draft.ts)). Logged meal items keep a link to their USDA food and the food table keeps each FNDDS code ([schema.ts](../src/db/schema.ts); [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md)), so a meal's home-cooked cost is a database join, not an AI call, and a cost term in the score is a small, testable change. The Premium offer to GLP-1 users already promises "protein-first meal ideas" that nothing yet delivers, and the second audience, people leaving MyFitnessPal and Lose It! over paywalls, is price-conscious by definition ([PLAN.md](../PLAN.md)). Everything else money needs is missing: the code has no recipes, shopping lists, grocery prices or pantry, and pack sizes exist only for scanned products; FNDDS portions are eating portions, not things a shop sells; household sharing sits on the "Not doing for now" list; and in the menu pipeline "priced" already means nutrition, so money needs the word "cost" ([PLAN.md](../PLAN.md); [menus.ts](../src/shared/menus.ts)). The honest scope is one person's day and week, not a family's dinners.

| Layer | TapCook today | Weight Class's version | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it plans for | Households: a servings counter, a kid-friendly toggle, one subscription per home | One person's day, then week; family plans stay on the "Not doing" list | Fits the one-diary account and avoids a fight Weight Class cannot win |
| Meals | Dinners only, from a catalogue reviewers put at 9–12 after filters | The person's own meals across the whole day, plus a small human-edited starter set of low-cost, protein-first meals built from USDA foods for people without seven logged days | Plan tomorrow needs history; a person's own meals never feel like a stranger's catalogue |

| Nutrition | None, and disclaimed | Calorie floors, protein target, GLP-1 protein-first weighting, fibre added to the draft numbers; calm mode hides money as well as calories | Exactly what TapCook's one-star reviewers ask for |
| Planner | On-device optimiser aiming at the budget | Plan tomorrow's score plus a cost term, the budget a goal that never cuts food; for a week, integer goal programming with a penalty for part-used packs | Thrifty Food Plan and goal-programming evidence; eating-disorder guardrails |
| AI | None at run time; recipes suspected of being AI-written | None in step one; later, behind the consent screen, drafting recipe steps for a person to edit and turning wishes into filters, with cooking temperatures and allergen flags from fixed tables; never a number, never budget, ZIP or goals in a prompt | Models miss energy and prices, and check better than they write |

| Paywall | 19 questions, then a hard paywall; the monthly plan bills at once | Cost lines free; budget planning inside Premium with Plan tomorrow; the price shown before any questions; no rating prompt before value | No second "pay to save" decision; the Cal AI precedent |

The order of work follows the evidence. First comes a spec in its own file, as What to order has, and a price table: load Purchase to Plate, carry it to the current month with CPI sub-indexes, and measure its error against a sample of real receipts and Open Prices barcodes before showing a single number, as What to order's pilot did when its estimated dishes missed by a median 19–20% ([WHAT-TO-ORDER.md](../WHAT-TO-ORDER.md)); foods without a price get none rather than a guess. Step one then ships visibly in a reviewed build, after the `app-store-review` skill's checks: a cost line on meals, a budget option in Plan tomorrow that says kindly when the floors cost more, a protein-per-dollar note in the weekly suggestion, and a Sources topic citing ERS, BLS and the GLP-1 advisory. Step two, the week plan, the starter set and the whole-pack list, earns its place only if people use step one, in the spirit of the plan's research slots that are "built only if the check passes" ([PLAN.md](../PLAN.md)). Step three, a retailer hand-off or Kroger's live prices, waits for an owner decision on commission, which sits awkwardly beside the public "No ads" promise and the plan's "no affiliate deals" stance on peptides ([landing page](../legal/index.html); [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md)); if taken, it sends items only, discloses the commission and never ranks by it. The 2021–23 Purchase to Plate cycle, due no earlier than spring 2027, should link to the same FNDDS 2021–2023 codes Weight Class uses and lift coverage when it lands ([ERS Purchase to Plate](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/purchase-to-plate)).

Beating TapCook then follows from its own reviews. The 41% who resent the paywall meet a price shown before any questions and a cost line that costs nothing; the 15% tired of the same dinners plan from their own meals; the sceptics of TapCook's prices get a named source, a date, a range and their own receipts; and the 9% who want calories, protein or GLP-1 help get the floors and weighting Weight Class already runs. Weight Class should not try to beat TapCook where it is strong, on families of six and TikTok budget challenges. Its marketing should sell protein on a budget, keep prices out of the name, subtitle and screenshots, and brief any creators to disclose payment on screen and aloud and to make no savings or weight claim the company cannot prove.

## Conclusion

TapCook's lesson is that the budget-planning leader wins without AI. Its product is a catalogue, an optimiser and a table of estimated prices wrapped in a TikTok creator machine, while the imitators that lean on generation are the ones reviewers accuse of "poorly AI-generated recipes". The value in this category is arithmetic and trust, which is where language models are weakest and where Weight Class's rule that code ranks and AI reads is already strongest. The second lesson is that the step that looked hardest turned out to be free: the federal government publishes national average prices keyed to the very food codes Weight Class stores, which turns TapCook's undisclosed method into a citable one and removes the licensing wall that shaped the MenuFit build. A tracker holds the same structural edge here as it did with restaurants, because only an app that already knows what someone eats can price their own meals rather than a stranger's catalogue.

The open question is demand inside Weight Class's own audience, not supply. GLP-1 users already spend less on groceries, so protein per dollar may matter more to the paywall-weary MyFitnessPal and Lose It! leavers than to the GLP-1 users Weight Class serves first; TapCook's revenue rests on third-party estimates; 2017–18 prices carried forward by CPI have never been checked against 2026 shelves; and the Washington and FTC readings await counsel. Each can be tested cheaply before a week plan exists, which is why the cost line should ship first and the shopping list only if people use it.
