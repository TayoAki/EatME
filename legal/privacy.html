2. Information we collect

We collect information you give us, information created when you use the Service, and technical information that your device sends when it connects to our server.

Account information

You create an EatME account with your first name, your email address and a password. Sign-in is handled by our own server with Better Auth, an open-source sign-in library that runs as part of our server software, so no third-party sign-in service is involved. We store your name, your email address and an identifier for your account.

We never store your password itself. Your password is sent to our server over an encrypted connection and turned into a salted, one-way hash (using the scrypt algorithm), and only that hash is stored. When you sign in, we check the password you enter against the hash.

When you sign up, we email you a 6-digit code to verify your email address, and if you forget your password, you can ask for a code to choose a new one. Codes are stored on our server only as a one-way hash, work for 10 minutes and for a few attempts, and choosing a new password signs you out on your other devices. We send these emails through Resend (see Section 6). We only send emails you ask for or need for your account, never marketing emails. If you lose access to your account, you can also contact us at [Contact Email].

Sign-in sessions

When you sign in, we create a session so that you stay signed in. A random session token is stored in your device's secure storage (the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore) and on our server. On our server, the session is stored together with its expiry date and with the IP address and user agent of the request that created it (the user agent is a short technical description of the app or browser that made the request, which can include your device's operating system version). The app sends the token with each request so that our server knows the request comes from your account. A session expires after 30 days without use, and signing out ends it.

Onboarding answers and your plan

To create your plan, we ask for your gender, date of birth, height, current weight, goal (lose, maintain or gain weight), target weight, activity level, weekly pace and diet type (for example, vegetarian), as well as whether you prefer metric or imperial units. We also store your device's time zone so that your days, history and streaks follow your local time.

From these answers we create your plan: daily targets for calories, protein, carbohydrates and fat, a short summary, and a note of whether the plan was generated by AI or by a standard formula. Your daily fiber and water goals start at the official recommendations for your age and sex. If you change any of your daily goals in the app, we store the goals you choose together with the targets your plan suggested, so that you can go back to them. While you complete onboarding, your answers are kept on your device and are saved to your account when you sign up. To show you a plan before you sign up, your answers are also sent to our server and, from there, to our AI providers (see Section 4). At that point, they are not linked to an account.

Meal photos and your meal log

When you scan a meal, we collect the photo you take or choose from your gallery. For each meal we store the photo, the meal name, the estimated calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat and fiber, how confident the estimate is, the portion size you pick, whether you marked the meal as a favourite, the time it was logged, the status of its analysis and any changes you make to those values. You can add a short note to a photo (for example, how it was cooked), which we store with the meal. Instead of a photo, you can describe a meal in words; we store that description with the meal. You can also photograph a nutrition label on food packaging; we store that photo, the serving size printed on it and the number of servings you choose. You can also scan a product's barcode or pick a food from our food database search; we store the product or food, its barcode and the amount you choose. When you log a meal again or copy a day, we store a copy of those meals, including a copy of their photos. If a photo does not show food or cannot be analyzed, we delete the photo and keep only a short note about what happened (see Section 4). We use the days on which you logged meals to show your history and to calculate your streak.

Water log

If you log drinks, we store the amount and the time of each entry so that we can show your daily total. You can remove an entry at any time.

GLP-1 mode

If you turn on GLP-1 mode, we store the medicine you pick, how often you take it and your dose day. When you log a dose, we store the time, the dose and injection site you enter and your note. When you log how you feel, we store the side effects you pick, how strong they were, your note and the time. This is health information: we use it only to show you your schedule and history, we never use it for advertising or share it with AI providers, and EatME never gives dosing advice. Turning GLP-1 mode off keeps your history; "Delete my GLP-1 data" in the app, or deleting your account, removes it permanently.

Weight history

Your weight history starts with the weight you entered during onboarding. Each time you log your weight, or change it in Personal details, we store the weight and the day. We use it to show your history and progress chart, and your latest weight becomes your current weight in your profile. You can remove any entry, and deleting your account removes all of them.

Supplements

If you add supplements, we store the name you give each one, the vitamin and mineral amounts per dose that you enter, how often you take it and the days on which you tick it as taken (with the dose as it was that day). We use this only to show your list and to add those amounts to your daily vitamins and minerals. We do not share it with AI providers, and EatME never gives dosing advice. Removing a supplement hides it from your list but keeps the days you already ticked; deleting your account removes all of it permanently.

Apple Health and Health Connect

If you turn on Apple Health (iPhone) or Health Connect (Android) in the app, the app writes the calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat and fiber of the meals you log, and the drinks you log, to that app on your device. EatME only writes this data: it does not read anything from Apple Health or Health Connect, and our servers never receive data from them. What is stored there is managed by Apple or Google under their terms; you can delete it in those apps. Turning sync off stops new writes.

Reminders

Reminders are scheduled by the app on your device. Your reminder choices stay on your device; we do not receive them, and we do not use push notification services to send them.

Feedback and messages

Feedback you send from the app is delivered to us through Sentry (see below). We receive your message and, if you choose to add them, your email address and a screenshot. A screenshot contains whatever is on your screen at that moment, so please check it before sending. If you email us, we receive your email address and the content of your message.

Diagnostic and technical information

To keep EatME reliable, the app uses Sentry, an error monitoring service, when crash reporting is enabled in the version of the app you use. In that case, Sentry collects:

  • Crash reports and errors: what went wrong and where, with technical details such as your device model, operating system and app version.
  • Diagnostic logs: short records of important events in the app, such as a failed upload or a completed meal analysis. These can include limited details, such as a meal's calorie estimate or your goal type.
  • Performance data: how long screens and network requests take, collected for a sample of sessions.
  • Session replays: for a sample of sessions, and for sessions in which an error occurs, a visual reconstruction of the screens you used and how you interacted with them. In the production app, all text and images are masked in replays, so they do not show your meals, photos, numbers or other on-screen content.

Diagnostic data is linked to your EatME account ID and may include your IP address.

In addition, whenever the app or your browser connects to our server, or the app loads one of your meal photos from our storage, standard technical information, such as your IP address, the time of the request, the address requested and the user agent, is processed to handle the request. Railway, our hosting provider, keeps short-lived logs of these requests and of important events that our server records, such as a completed meal analysis or an error. These logs can include limited details, such as an account or meal identifier or a calorie estimate. Our server also holds IP addresses briefly in memory to limit how often sign-in attempts and plan requests can be made, which protects your account and the Service against abuse.

What we do not collect

We do not collect your precise location, your contacts, audio recordings, advertising identifiers or payment details, and we do not read data from other health or fitness apps. We do not use your photos to identify people.

4. How AI processing works

EatME uses artificial intelligence ("AI") in two places. Our server accesses OpenAI's models through OpenRouter, a service that routes requests to AI model providers. The app never sends your data to an AI provider directly, and the requests our server sends do not include your name, email address, account ID or IP address.

Creating your plan

When you finish the onboarding questions, the app sends your answers to our server, which sends them to an AI model through OpenRouter, together with instructions for calculating daily nutrition targets. The model receives your gender, age, height, weight, goal, target weight, weekly pace, activity level and diet. It receives your age, which our server calculates from your date of birth, not your date of birth itself. This happens before you create an account, so the request is not linked to an account. The plan is saved to your account when you sign up. If the AI service is unavailable, we calculate your plan with a standard formula (the Mifflin-St Jeor equation) instead.

Analyzing your meals

When you scan a meal, the app resizes the photo (to at most 1,280 pixels on its longest side) and uploads it to our server. Our server stores the photo in a private storage bucket hosted by Railway and sends a copy of it, as image data inside the request, to an AI vision model through OpenRouter. The model returns a name for the meal, estimates of its calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat and fiber, and how confident the estimate is. If the model decides that the photo does not show food or a drink, the photo is deleted right after the analysis, and only a short note on why it was not treated as food stays with the meal record. If the analysis cannot be completed, the photo is deleted as well. Photos of the meals you log are kept until you delete the meal or your account. If you added a note to the photo, the note is sent with it. Photos of nutrition labels are sent in higher resolution so that the model can read the printed values.

When you describe a meal in words, our server sends your description (without your name or email address) to an AI language model through OpenRouter, which returns the same kind of estimate as for a photo.

The AI also lists the foods it sees with their weight. To look them up in the USDA food database (a public database stored on our server), our server sends those food names and weights, together with database entries that could match, to an AI language model through OpenRouter, which picks the matching entries. No photo, name or other account information is included in that request.

Barcodes and food search (no AI)

When you scan or type a barcode, our server looks the number up in its own copy of product data first and, when needed, asks Open Food Facts (a public, open food database) and, if configured, USDA FoodData Central for the product. Only the barcode number is sent, from our server, never your name, email address or other account information, and the product data is kept on our server so that the next lookup is answered there. Searching foods and logging a barcode or a food from the search do not use AI.

What the AI providers do with your data

OpenRouter and OpenAI process the content we send only to return results to us. We do not permit them to use your content to train or improve their models, and our OpenRouter account is configured accordingly, with training on our requests and logging of their content turned off. Where other settings are available to us, we choose the ones that keep your content for the shortest time. Under their own terms, they may keep content for a limited period, for example to detect abuse.

Please do not include people, faces, documents, screens or other personal information in your meal photos.

AI results can be wrong

AI estimates can be inaccurate. Portion sizes, hidden ingredients such as oil or sugar, and the angle or lighting of a photo can all affect the result. Always review the estimates and correct them when needed. See Section 15.

We keep personal information only for as long as we need it for the purposes described in this policy:

10. Security

We use technical and organizational measures designed to protect your information, including:

  • encryption in transit: HTTPS (TLS) for connections between the app or your browser and our server, and encrypted connections between our server, our database, our photo storage and our other providers;
  • passwords stored only as salted, one-way hashes (scrypt), never as the password itself;
  • sign-in checks on every request, so that your profile and meals are available only to your account, and sessions that expire after 30 days without use and that you can end at any time by signing out;
  • limits on repeated sign-in attempts and other requests, to slow down password guessing and abuse;
  • meal photos kept in a private storage bucket that is encrypted at rest and is never made public;
  • keeping secret keys on our server, never in the app, and limiting who can access production systems;

The app shows your meal photos through signed links that our server creates only for your account and that expire after 6 hours. We do not publish these links, but anyone who obtained a link could open the photo until it expires. This is another reason not to include people or personal documents in your meal photos.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, so we cannot guarantee absolute security. If a security incident affects your personal information, we will notify you and the relevant authorities where the law requires it.

15. Health information disclaimer

EatME provides estimates for general information and wellness purposes. It is not a medical device, and it does not provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.

The calorie and nutrient values in the app are estimated by AI from photos and may be inaccurate or incomplete. Your plan is a general target based on the information you provide and does not take your medical history into account.
